Halloween ends, but a pumpkin's story can have another chapter.

WSAZ reports that The Pumpkin House in Kenova, West Virginia, is donating its 3,000 jack-o-lanterns to farmers for livestock, with collection beginning November 2.

This playful sketch imagines a curious farmyard welcome for the autumn leftovers. It is an illustration inspired by the story, rather than a record of a particular farm or animal.

There is something lovely about a community tradition finding a second purpose after the lights go out. A small seasonal reminder to notice what might still have value.

Source: WSAZ, November 2 report.
